# Product principles

Agreed 2026-10-15, when direction C ("Arris", Quiet Product) was chosen. Every
screen is designed against these. When two of them pull against each other, the
order they appear in decides.

## The idea in one line

**One obvious path for everyone, and real depth for the manager that is there when
he wants it and never in the way when he doesn't.**

Apple's strength is that you always know the next step and you are never
overwhelmed. Its weakness is that when you want to do something slightly
different, the path stops. This product keeps the first and refuses the second.

## 1. The floor: nothing to learn

- **One question per screen.** My Jobs answers "what am I doing next?" and nothing
  else competes with it.
- **One primary action per screen,** and it is obvious. Secondary actions are
  available but quieter.
- **No menus to hunt through.** If a joiner needs it daily, it is on the screen.
  If they need it weekly, it is one tap away. If they never need it, it is not there.
- **Readable at arm's length.** Calm never means small or faint. Stage labels, job
  numbers and actions are sized for a tablet on a bench, read with gloves on.
- **Every action can be undone,** so nobody is afraid to tap.

## 2. The manager: saves him time, never costs it

He uses it more than anyone, so his time is the measure of whether it works.

- **A "Today" view** shows only what needs him: drawing queries, materials holding
  jobs up, jobs at risk of being late, QC waiting for sign-off. The aim is a daily
  check in about five minutes.
- **Creating a job takes under a minute,** with smart defaults, templates for repeat
  work (a set of fire doors, a reception desk) and a cutting list imported, not
  typed.
- **Fast for someone who knows it, simple for someone who doesn't.** On the laptop:
  a command bar (Cmd K) to jump to any job or action, keyboard shortcuts, bulk
  actions, inline editing, filters he can save. None of it is needed to use the app.
  All of it is there once he wants it.
- **Nothing is a dead end.** If the app thinks something is unusual (moving to CNC
  with materials outstanding), it warns and explains, then lets him decide. It does
  not block him except where blocking is the point, like the drawing gate.

## 3. He can fix things himself

No developer for simple changes. The manager can, in a settings area:

- Add, remove and edit people, their roles, and reset PINs
- Edit the materials list and suppliers
- Set default hours per stage for each kind of job
- Create and edit job templates
- Edit the QC checklist wording

The structure of the app (the stages and the gates) stays fixed. That is where
safety lives, and changing it is a decision, not a setting.

## 4. Power without clutter

The test for any new button: who needs it, how often, and where? Daily for
everyone goes on the screen. Occasional or manager-only goes in a quiet "more"
menu, a keyboard shortcut or the command bar. Rare goes in settings. If nobody can
name who needs it, it does not ship.
